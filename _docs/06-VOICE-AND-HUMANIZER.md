# Voice & Humanizer Rules

The first draft read like AI output: slogan headlines, numbered everything, identical cards, 147 em dashes. These rules override the phrasing in the decks. They never override the **facts** in `01-CONTENT-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md`. Rewrite the wording freely; don't add, drop or inflate a fact.

## Who is talking
A senior engineer at Hashmark explaining the work to a founder or CTO. Plain, specific, a little dry. Confident because of what we've shipped, not because of adjectives.

## Copy rules
1. **Specific beats clever.** Name the thing: "a matching engine that fills user-to-user first and routes the rest to Binance, Coinbase, Bybit and B2broker", not "exchange-grade execution".
2. **No slogan pairs.** Ban "X, not Y" / "Proof, not promises" / "Deliverables, not decks". At most one contrast line per page, and only if it carries a real point.
3. **No fragment stacks.** No "Senior-led. Structured. Transparent." or "Map. Engage. Close. Compound." Write a sentence.
4. **Em dashes: rare.** Max one per paragraph, and none in headlines or short lines. Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses.
5. **Break the rule of three.** Lists can have two, four or five items. Don't pad to three.
6. **Banned words:** seamless, robust, cutting-edge, leverage, unlock, elevate, empower, journey, world-class, best-in-class, game-changer, synergy, holistic, next-level, "in today's fast-paced…", "we're passionate about".
7. **Headlines are statements someone would say out loud**, sentence case, usually under 10 words. No rhetorical "Why X?" headers.
8. **Vary rhythm.** Mix short and long sentences. Use "we" and "you". Contractions are fine.
9. **Be honest about limits.** "We don't give legal advice." "We can't get you listed; nobody honest can promise that."
10. **Don't repeat the same claim across sections.** Say "senior engineers only" once per page, well.

## Design rules
1. **Number only real sequences** (process steps). No section indices, no "01" on unordered cards.
2. **Small labels only where they help scanning.** Mono uppercase is for code-ish things: stack chips, domains, file-like metadata. Section labels are optional and short.
3. **Mix layouts.** Prose paragraphs, two-column text, tables, plain lists. Cards only when items are genuinely separate objects (case studies, people, engagement options).
4. **No decoration pretending to be content.** Real things beat ornaments: live client domains beat a glowing network graphic.
5. **Quiet interaction.** Flat buttons, no glow shadows, no count-up numbers, no pinging dots, no lift-on-hover on every card. One subtle fade-in is enough.
6. **Shorter pages.** Each page should make its point and end. Cut sections that repeat another section.
